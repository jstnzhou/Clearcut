The submission

A plantation in West Kalimantan asks to be approved.

It sends one thing a reviewer can check: the location of the plot. Every other claim on the form is the applicant’s own word.

14 Sep 2019

Before the cutoff, it was forest.

Unbroken canopy across the whole box. A river winds through the upper left. Mean NDVI 0.88.

31 Dec 2020

The date the law looks back to.

Forest turned into farmland after this day cannot supply the EU market. The duty starts in December 2026. The forest that counts is the forest of 2020.

Then, in one year, about 880 hectares.

Each red cell is a 30-metre pixel the Hansen Global Forest Change map records as lost. Almost all of the post-2020 loss on this plot falls in 2023.

Method

Four records, laid on the same ground.

  1. 01

    Two pictures

    The clearest Sentinel-2 scene before the cutoff, and the clearest one now. Same box, same season where possible.

  2. 02

    The loss year

    Hansen Global Forest Change marks the year each 30 m pixel lost its trees, through 2024.

  3. 03

    What it became

    A vision model reads the current image and outlines regions that look converted: blocks, roads, rows.

  4. 04

    The cross-check

    A region is confirmed only when the loss map shows substantial clearing inside it after 2020. Anything else stays inconclusive.

Limits

What a check can say, and what it never will.

Says

  • This plot shows post-2020 tree-cover loss consistent with plantation conversion.
  • This region is inconclusive and needs a person.
  • No post-2020 loss and no conversion in the imagery: consistent with the applicant’s claim.

Never says

  • Non-compliant, illegal, or banned. Those are findings for an authority.
  • A supplier score. A check is about one piece of ground, not a company.
  • Detected in 2026. The loss map ends in 2024. Newer images show what the land is now.
  • An enhanced image. Every picture is the satellite scene as delivered.

Public by design

People who live with that forest can read the same pack.

Approval rests on the images and the loss map, not on the applicant’s description of the land. Every check carries its scene IDs, dates and cloud cover, so anyone can pull the same data and see the same thing.
