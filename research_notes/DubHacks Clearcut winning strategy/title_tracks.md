## List every title track with prompt, prizes, and judging notes

### Takeaway
There are exactly four title tracks. A team must submit to one of them. The three DubHacks tracks (Community Kitchen, Cook Smarter, Chef’s Choice) share the same hardware prize menu and the same 30/30/20/20 rubric. Visa: Reimagine Shopping is the sponsored title track, pays $5,000 cash split across the team, and is scored on a separate commerce rubric with virtual judging.

### Cited Findings
- “Reminder that you’re required to submit your project to exactly one title track.” The Hacker Guide restates this as “3 DubHacks title tracks and 1 sponsored title track.” — [Title tracks](https://dubhacks.notion.site/26-Title-Tracks-bbaff32c1d7e8308be7d8185bd983487); [Hacker Guide](https://dubhacks.notion.site/The-DubHacks-26-Hacker-Guide-15dff32c1d7e83748aac8154036aa658)

Most of our tracks are very open ended so you can build anything you can think of!” — [Devpost](https://dubhacks-2026.devpost.com/)

## Compare Community Kitchen vs Cook Smarter for Clearcut

### Takeaway
Enter Cook Smarter. The official Cook Smarter prompt is a workflow and decision tool for getting work done with less effort. Clearcut’s stated user is a buyer checking one supplier plot. Community Kitchen does name sustainability, but its prompt is about people thriving through civic, health, equity, and community tools, and a buyer due-diligence demo is a weaker reading of that prompt.

### Cited Findings
- Community Kitchen, title-tracks page, full prompt: “Build technology that helps people thrive. From accessibility and healthcare to civic tech, sustainability, emergency response, and community support, create solutions that make everyday life more connected, equitable, and resilient.” — [Title tracks](https://dubhacks.notion.site/26-Title-Tracks-bbaff32c1d7e8308be7d8185bd983487)
- Community Kitchen topics on that page: “accessibility and assistive tech, mental health and wellbeing, emergency response and public safety, civic tech, equity, etc.” The word “sustainability” is in the prompt sentence, not in that topic list. — [Title tracks](https://dubhacks.notion.site/26-Title-Tracks-bbaff32c1d7e8308be7d8185bd983487)
- Community Kitchen topics in the Hacker Guide, which is the only place “sustainability & equity” appears as a topic bullet, and which also imports productivity language: “Accessibility and assistive tech, mental health and wellbeing, emergency response and public safety, civic tech, crisis management, equity, cultural connection, social good, productivity and coordination tools, sustainability & equity.” — [Hacker Guide](https://dubhacks.notion.site/The-DubHacks-26-Hacker-Guide-15dff32c1d7e83748aac8154036aa658)
- Cook Smarter, title-tracks page, full prompt: “Make work easier and life more efficient. Build AI agents, automation, productivity tools, planning systems, developer tools, or workflows that reduce friction and help people accomplish more with less effort.” Topics: “productivity tools, AI agents, automation, scheduling and planning, logistics, operations, business efficiency, data management, etc.” — [Title tracks](https://dubhacks.notion.site/26-Title-Tracks-bbaff32c1d7e8308be7d8185bd983487)
- Cook Smarter Hacker Guide topics add the closest official phrase to this product: “collaboration tools, knowledge management, smart systems, developer tools, data management, decision support systems.” — [Hacker Guide](https://dubhacks.notion.site/The-DubHacks-26-Hacker-Guide-15dff32c1d7e83748aac8154036aa658)
- Track-choice guidance: “3. Prioritize track applicability. The most important factor is how well your project actually fits a track.” Then “4. Decide what you’re optimizing for”: “Impact: solving a real problem for real people”; “Automation: making a process faster or easier”; “Innovation: doing something new or technically creative.” — [Picking a Track](https://dubhacks.notion.site/p/3faff32c1d7e8089ac4ad35c334c1457)
- Applicability is 30% of the main rubric. Excellent: “Project goes above and beyond in its applicability to the chosen track. The project serves as an exemplary demonstration of the track's focus.” Fair: “Project shows a superficial connection to the chosen track. Some elements might align, but the overall relation is unclear or weak.” — [Official Judges Rubric](https://dubhacks.notion.site/p/154ff32c1d7e8304a76b011636f6732f)

### Inferences
- Clearcut, as specified (a buyer opens a supplier plot and gets before/after Sentinel-2, Hansen loss years, a vision description of forest-to-plantation conversion, and boxes checked against Hansen), is a decision-support workflow. That matches Cook Smarter’s “workflows that reduce friction,” “operations,” “business efficiency,” “data management,” and the Hacker Guide’s “decision support systems.”
- The sustainability subject matter is real, and Community Kitchen’s prompt does say “sustainability.” The target user in the brief is a commercial buyer, not a community, civic, health, or emergency user. Under the published fair band, a judge can call that a superficial connection: the domain is sustainability, the product is supplier checking.
- The Hacker Guide also lists “productivity and coordination tools” under Community Kitchen, so the two tracks overlap on paper. That overlap is why the demo framing matters. A pitch about community forest guardians fits the Community Kitchen sentence “helps people thrive.” A pitch about a buyer finishing a supplier check fits Cook Smarter’s “accomplish more with less effort.”
- Picking a Track’s three optimization labels line up with the three DubHacks tracks: Impact with Community Kitchen, Automation with Cook Smarter, Innovation with Chef’s Choice. This project’s brief is a faster check, which is the Automation line.
- Chef’s Choice is a weaker third option. Its prompt asks for technology that is “imaginative, expressive, and fun,” with topics in UI, games, art, and music. A compliance evidence tool can be well designed, but “fun” is not what the brief describes. — prompt quoted above from [Title tracks](https://dubhacks.notion.site/26-Title-Tracks-bbaff32c1d7e8308be7d8185bd983487)
- Visa is a poor fit. The prompt requires a “commerce experience” across discovery, checkout, payments, loyalty, or post-purchase, and the Visa rubric scores “Commerce Impact” on engagement, conversion, loyalty, or merchant outcomes. A deforestation evidence view is not that journey. — [Hacker Guide](https://dubhacks.notion.site/The-DubHacks-26-Hacker-Guide-15dff32c1d7e83748aac8154036aa658); [VISA Judging Rubric](https://dubhacks.notion.site/p/3efff32c1d7e809d9063de7d7157d957)

### Gaps
- No organizer, judge, or past-winner quote says which of these two tracks a sustainability or procurement project should enter.
- The Hacker Guide topic lists and the title-tracks page topic lists differ. It is not stated which list judges are given at the table.

## What is the main rubric, and are there extra criteria?

### Takeaway
For Community Kitchen, Cook Smarter, and Chef’s Choice, judges score four criteria on a 1–8 scale: Functionality 30%, Track Applicability 30%, Design 20%, Creativity 20%. Excellent functionality means the project works in the demo. Visa replaces that rubric with its own five commerce criteria.

### Cited Findings
- Devpost judging criteria, which match the Notion weights: “Functionality — Code Functionality and Quality of Execution (30%)”; “Track Applicability — How related projects are to their chosen track (30%)”; “Design — User Interface/Aesthetics (20%)”; “Creativity — Unique/New Solution to a Problem with Little Weaknesses (20%).” Devpost says to use the Hacker Guide rubric for the point breakdown. — [Devpost](https://dubhacks-2026.devpost.com/)
- Official rubric table, each row marked “(8 Points)” plus the weight. Bands are Insufficient (1–2), Fair (3–4), Good (5–6), Excellent (7–8). — [Official Judges Rubric](https://dubhacks.notion.site/p/154ff32c1d7e8304a76b011636f6732f)
- Functionality excellent: “The project successfully works to implement the solution, demonstrating robust code functionality and high quality of code execution.” Good: imaginable with “only minor modifications.” Fair: “substantial modifications.” Insufficient: “not possible to imagine the elements of the project or the underlying code working together.” — [Official Judges Rubric](https://dubhacks.notion.site/p/154ff32c1d7e8304a76b011636f6732f)
- Design excellent: “The user interface is very easy to understand and looks very professional aesthetically.” Good: “easy to understand and is aesthetically pleasing.” Fair: “visually understandable but lacks creativity or sophistication.” Insufficient: “lacks any aesthetic appeal and confuses users.” — [Official Judges Rubric](https://dubhacks.notion.site/p/154ff32c1d7e8304a76b011636f6732f)
- Creativity excellent: “Complex and seemingly addresses all angles of the potential technological and contextual constraints in a very unique manner.” Good: “Moderately complex… moderately stands out.” Fair: “Simple… lacks distinction.” Insufficient: “Overly simplistic and merely replicates existing approaches.” — [Official Judges Rubric](https://dubhacks.notion.site/p/154ff32c1d7e8304a76b011636f6732f)
- Track applicability excellent / good / fair / insufficient quoted in the previous section. Weight 30%. — [Official Judges Rubric](https://dubhacks.notion.site/p/154ff32c1d7e8304a76b011636f6732f)
- Extra process criteria, not extra score rows: video required or the team is not judged; keep the live pitch under 3 minutes; at least one member present; code linked on Devpost before the deadline; 70% of code written during the event (at most 30% pre-existing). — [Submissions and Judging](https://dubhacks.notion.site/p/14eff32c1d7e837793e2018a30a234cb); [Hacker Guide](https://dubhacks.notion.site/The-DubHacks-26-Hacker-Guide-15dff32c1d7e83748aac8154036aa658)
- Visa does not use those four weights. Its extra criteria are Innovation, User Experience, Technical Execution, Commerce Impact, and Trust & Responsibility, with the weight conflict noted above. — [VISA Judging Rubric](https://dubhacks.notion.site/p/3efff32c1d7e809d9063de7d7157d957)

### Inferences
- A precomputed demo can still hit the excellent functionality band if the judge can run the buyer flow in the room and the code is real. The excellent line is “successfully works,” not “runs live inference.” The good band is for a demo that still needs minor fixes. A slideshow of screenshots with no working path falls toward fair or insufficient.
- Design is 20% and the excellent line says “very professional.” For this project that means the plot, the two image dates, the loss year, the model boxes, and the Hansen cross-check should be readable in under a minute, because the pitch is capped at about 3 minutes.
- Creativity’s insufficient band punishes “merely replicates existing approaches.” Showing Sentinel-2 and Hansen side by side is a known pattern. The cross-check between Perceptron boxes and Hansen years is the part that can be described as addressing a real constraint (a vision box that disagrees with the loss map) rather than a clone of Global Forest Watch.

### Gaps
- The rubric does not say whether the 8-point scale is multiplied by the percentage or whether judges enter 8 points per row and organizers apply the weights later. Both Devpost and the rubric print “(8 Points)” next to every row, including the 30% rows and the 20% rows.
- No score sheet was found for side tracks. Perceptron AI prizes are marked “COMING SOON,” so there is no published Perceptron rubric to add. — [Prizes](https://dubhacks.notion.site/p/7f2ff32c1d7e83cbb22981e0c329c033)

## Which track has less competition or a clearer fit? Any sponsor or judge hints?

### Takeaway
Cook Smarter is the clearer fit. No official source publishes how many teams enter each title track, so “less competition” is not an evidenced claim. The only named judge is Visa’s Lisa Kapil, and that track’s prompt does not fit this project. Perceptron is a side-track sponsor with prizes still unpublished, not a title track.

Devpost, when fetched, showed on the order of 230 participants and “$9,200 in cash” on the header, while the event site advertises “1,100+ Participants,” “$44,000+ In Prizes,” and “1,500+ Projects.” — [Devpost](https://dubhacks-2026.devpost.com/); [dh26.dubhacks.co](https://dh26.dubhacks.co/)

### Inferences
- Because prizes are identical across the three DubHacks title tracks, there is no published incentive to pick the more crowded social-good track. Teams chasing cash are pointed at Visa. That is a reason Visa may draw prize-seeking teams, and a reason not to follow them: Commerce Impact is 15% of a rubric this project does not satisfy.
- It is plausible that sustainability projects cluster in Community Kitchen because that prompt is the only one that says “sustainability.” That would make Cook Smarter less crowded for a forest project. No submission counts, Discord counts, or organizer statement supports that. Treat it as a guess.
- If the team also submits the Perceptron side track (one of up to three sponsored side tracks), Perceptron judges walk to the table. That does not change the title-track choice. The title track is still scored on the four-criterion rubric.

### Gaps
- No per-track registration or historical winner counts for 2026, and no 2025 Community Kitchen or Cook Smarter winner write-ups were found in this pass.
- Perceptron prize amounts, prompt, and rubric were still “COMING SOON” on the prizes page.
- Devpost’s ~230 participants and the event site’s 1,100+ / 1,500+ figures conflict. Neither is a title-track field size. The Devpost number was a live counter on 17 October 2026, before the Sunday deadline.

## What would a winning project in each track look like?

### Takeaway
These sketches are inferences from the prompts and rubrics, not official winner profiles. A winning Cook Smarter project is a working decision tool a person finishes a real task in. A winning Community Kitchen project is aimed at a person or community that needs the tool to live more safely or fairly. A winning Chef’s Choice project is something people want to play with. A winning Visa project is a generative shopping flow that could move a purchase.

### Cited Findings
- The prompts, topic lists, and rubrics quoted in the sections above are the only basis for the sketches. No 2026 winning projects exist yet. The event runs 17–18 October 2026. — [dh26.dubhacks.co](https://dh26.dubhacks.co/)
- Excellent bars used below: works in the room; exemplary track fit; professional UI; complex and specific about constraints. ### Inferences
- **Cook Smarter winner.** A judge watches one user finish a job they already have. The demo opens on a task, not a model card: a buyer has a plot and a supplier claim, and in one pass the screen answers whether that plot was cleared, in which years, and whether the vision boxes agree with Hansen. The AI is an agent or decision step inside that workflow (describe the conversion, box the change, flag disagreement), which is what “AI agents,” “workflows that reduce friction,” and “decision support systems” ask for. The UI is plain enough to trust in three minutes. The creative point is the constraint the tool handles, such as a box that Hansen does not support, not a novel map style.
- **Community Kitchen winner.** A judge can name the person who is better off and the everyday harm that shrinks. The prompt’s examples are accessibility, healthcare, civic tech, emergency response, and community support, plus the Hacker Guide’s equity and social-good topics. A forest project wins here if the user is a community monitor, a local official, or a resident defending land, and the demo shows a decision that community can act on. A supplier-audit dashboard for a fictional buyer can still mention sustainability, but that reading sits closer to the fair band (“superficial connection”) unless the pitch is rebuilt around those users.
- **Chef’s Choice winner.** The project is the thing people gather around: a game, a creative tool, a striking interface, or an AI toy that is “imaginative, expressive, and fun.” Technical depth still has to work, but the track language rewards expression and play over a compliance outcome. An evidence tool would need to become an exploratory, beautiful way of seeing change, and even then it is arguing against “fun.”
- **Visa winner.** A shopper talks to or collaborates with a generative system and moves through at least one listed stage (discovery, comparison, personalization, checkout, payments, loyalty, post-purchase). The separate rubric wants a reimagined shopping experience, a polished flow, real AI in the product, a reason it would change conversion or loyalty, and a straight answer on privacy and trust. Cash is $5,000. A land-evidence tool does not become this by adding a buy button.
