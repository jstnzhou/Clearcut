# Clearcut product scope: user, claim, and EUDR date

Research date: 17 October 2026. Pipeline facts (Hansen through 2024, Sentinel-2, vision model, West Kalimantan demo plot, offline precompute, fictional supplier, no generated images) are treated as given and are not re-researched here.

## What is the EUDR application date as of October 2026, and who does it cover?

### Takeaway
The team’s rumor is right on the day and wrong on the scope. As of 17 October 2026, the binding date is 30 December 2026 for the main obligations, including large and medium operators. Operators who are natural persons or micro or small undertakings, and who already had that status by 31 December 2024, generally wait until 30 June 2027, except for timber products already covered by the EU Timber Regulation, which stay on 30 December 2026. The law has already slipped twice (from 30 December 2024, then from 30 December 2025). The Commission’s May 2026 review said it would not propose another change to the basic text, but a third political delay is still not impossible in the ten weeks left before 30 December 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Regulation (EU) 2023/1115 entered into force on 29 June 2023. Its main operator, trader, and competent-authority obligations were originally due on 30 December 2024. A first postponement, adopted in December 2024, moved that date to 30 December 2025. — [Council of the EU, 18 December 2025](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2025/12/18/deforestation-council-signs-off-targeted-revision-to-simplify-and-postpone-the-regulation/)
- Regulation (EU) 2025/2650 of 19 December 2025 postponed those obligations by another 12 months. Recital 15 says the delay was so third countries, Member States, operators, and traders could prepare. The replacement Article 38(2) says Articles 3 to 13, 16 to 24, 26, 31, and 32 apply from 30 December 2026. — [EUR-Lex, Regulation (EU) 2025/2650](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/2650/oj/eng); consolidated text dated 26 December 2025: [CELEX 02023R1115-20251226](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A02023R1115-20251226)
- Article 38(3) is narrower than “all SMEs.” Except for products in the Annex to the EU Timber Regulation (Regulation (EU) No 995/2010), operators who are natural persons or micro- or small undertakings under Directive 2013/34/EU, and who were established as such by 31 December 2024, have those same articles apply from 30 June 2027. Medium-sized operators are not in that sentence, so they fall on 30 December 2026. — [EUR-Lex, Regulation (EU) 2025/2650, new Article 38](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/2650/oj/eng)
- The Commission’s public page, still stating this split in October 2026, summarizes it as: large and medium operators, 30 December 2026; micro and small operators, 30 June 2027; micro and small operators already covered by the EU Timber Regulation, 30 December 2026. — [European Commission, Regulation on Deforestation-free products](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/topics/forests/deforestation/regulation-deforestation-free-products_en)
- The Commission’s implementation page uses slightly wider wording (“large and medium enterprises” on 30 December 2026, “most micro and small enterprises” on 30 June 2027, EUTR micro and small on 30 December 2026). The article itself says “operators,” not every micro or small company in the chain. — [Green Forum, roles and responsibilities](https://green-forum.ec.europa.eu/nature-and-biodiversity/implementing-eu-deforestation-regulation-eudr/roles-and-responsibilities_en)
- DG Trade’s Access2Markets note puts downstream operators and traders of all sizes, plus large and medium operators, plus micro and small enterprises for EUTR-annex products, on 30 December 2026. Its table then says 30 June 2027 is for “operators who are natural persons and micro-enterprises,” which drops “small” even though the same page’s prose and Article 38(3) include small undertakings. Prefer the regulation over that table cell. — [Access2Markets](https://trade.ec.europa.eu/access-to-markets/en/news/delay-until-december-2026-and-other-developments-implementation-eudr-regulation)
- Who owes what, after the December 2025 simplification: the upstream operator (the person who first places a covered product on the EU market, or exports it) must do due diligence and file a due diligence statement. A micro or small primary operator in a low-risk country can file one simplified declaration instead, and may use a postal address instead of coordinates. Downstream operators and traders do not run due diligence or file statements. They keep supplier and buyer information for five years and must tell buyers and authorities if they learn a product might not comply. Only large (non-SME) downstream operators and traders must register in the Information System, and they verify upstream due diligence only if they have a substantiated concern. — [Green Forum, roles](https://green-forum.ec.europa.eu/nature-and-biodiversity/implementing-eu-deforestation-regulation-eudr/roles-and-responsibilities_en); [Green Forum, due diligence](https://green-forum.ec.europa.eu/nature-and-biodiversity/deforestation-regulation-implementation/understand-due-diligence_en)
- Parliament’s December 2025 deal press release used “large operators and traders” for 30 December 2026 and “small operators — private individuals and micro- or small enterprises” for 30 June 2027. That press language is looser than Article 38 as adopted, which puts medium operators with the December date and limits the June date to operators. — [European Parliament, 4 December 2025](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/news/en/press-room/20251201IPR31711/deforestation-law-deal-with-council-to-postpone-and-simplify-measures)
- On 4 May 2026 the Commission’s mandated simplification review (COM(2026) 191) said application was still “as of the end of 2026,” restated the 30 December 2026 / 30 June 2027 split, and said it did “not consider it appropriate to propose further amendments to the basic legal text,” citing legal certainty. — [COM(2026) 191](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=celex%3A52026DC0191); [Commission PDF](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/document/download/a3c5c3a0-232e-43c4-b0b8-1eecb1df45c7_en?filename=Report+from+the+Commission+to+the+Council+and+Parliament+on+the+EUDR.pdf)
- On 13 July 2026 Commissioner Jessika Roswall said the package was meant to give predictability “ahead of the Regulation's application” “at the end of 2026.” The same news item repeats 30 December 2026 for large and medium operators and for micro and small operators already under the EU Timber Regulation. New products added by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/2102 (soluble coffee, certain palm-oil derivatives, frozen cattle tongues) are a separate, later date: 30 December 2027. — [European Commission, 13 July 2026](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/news/commission-updates-product-scope-and-tools-support-eudr-2026-07-13_en); [Commission topic page](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/topics/forests/deforestation/regulation-deforestation-free-products_en)
- A 5 October 2026 trade-press briefing quotes a barrister that implementation in December 2026 is more likely than a third delay, and that it would be “foolish” to rule a third delay out because some Member States are still lobbying. An NGO (Fern) and WRI’s forest-governance director are quoted saying another delay would hurt credibility. This is opinion, not a legislative act. — [FoodNavigator, 5 October 2026](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/10/05/eudr-will-it-be-simplified-further/)
- A vendor explainer dated in its own text to 17 October 2026 says EUR-Lex then listed no proposal changing 30 December 2026 or 30 June 2027, and that the only open amendment it found (COM(2026) 661) concerned French Guiana. Treat that as a secondary cross-check, not as a substitute for the regulation. — [Polygon-one](https://polygon-one.com/en/eudr-current-status)

### Inferences
- Say on the demo slide: “Main EUDR duties: 30 December 2026. Most micro and small operators: 30 June 2027. Already delayed twice.” Do not say “large operators only.”
- The user who feels the date is the upstream operator who must file the statement, and the buyer who will be asked for plot coordinates before that filing. A downstream SME trader is the wrong hero for this demo: after December 2025 they mostly file a reference number, not a satellite case.
- Leave one line of uncertainty on the slide. The date is law today. It is not immune to a December political scramble of the kind that produced the 2024 and 2025 delays.

### Gaps
- This pass did not open the EUR-Lex procedure search itself on 17 October 2026. The “no pending date-change proposal” claim rests on the vendor cross-check plus the absence of any Commission page announcing a third postponement. If a proposal appeared after the July 2026 package, the Commission topic page fetched today does not mention it.
- Article 38(3) ties the June 2027 date to operators established as micro or small by 31 December 2024. What happens to a firm that crossed the size threshold in 2025 was not pinned down here.

## Who pays for plot-level checks, and what do they need in five minutes?

### Takeaway
The cheque is written by the company that has to stand behind a plot coordinate: the upstream operator, and in practice the commodity buyer or trader who already monitors mills and farms for its own no-deforestation promise. Auditors and NGOs use the same pictures, but they are not the primary customer of a five-minute demo. That customer needs one evidence pack for one plot, with a sentence they are allowed to say. They do not need a portfolio dashboard.

### Cited Findings
- The operator who places the product on the EU market or exports it must collect the coordinates of every plot where the commodity was produced. Plots over 4 hectares (except cattle) need a polygon. If they cannot collect every plot, they must not place the product. Remotely sensed images “may be used” to check whether that geolocation is linked to deforestation. The Commission does not require a named satellite product or a minimum resolution. — [Commission FAQ, sections 1.1, 1.8, 5.18](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/publications/faq-eudr-implementation_en) (FAQ file dated in the Commission listing as updated around April 2026, publication page 21 August 2026)
- FAQ 1.19: operators, non-SME downstream actors, and enforcing authorities “may cross-check the geolocation coordinates against satellite images or forest cover maps.” FAQ 1.20: Member State competent authorities check that products come from deforestation-free plots and were produced legally, including by checking due diligence statements. Article 18 explicitly allows “Earth observation data such as from the Copernicus programme” and other public or private sources. Authorities may do field audits in third countries only with that country’s agreement. — [Commission FAQ 1.19–1.21](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/publications/faq-eudr-implementation_en); [consolidated Regulation, Article 18](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A02023R1115-20251226)
- Risk mitigation under Article 11 may include extra documents, independent surveys or audits, and help to suppliers. Non-SME operators must have an independent audit function over their own due diligence system. That makes an auditor a reviewer of the file, not the person who first pays to scan every plot. — [Green Forum, due diligence](https://green-forum.ec.europa.eu/nature-and-biodiversity/deforestation-regulation-implementation/understand-due-diligence_en)
- Smallholders outside the EU are not themselves covered unless they place the product on the EU market. Buyers still ask them for plot geolocation. — [Green Forum, roles](https://green-forum.ec.europa.eu/nature-and-biodiversity/implementing-eu-deforestation-regulation-eudr/roles-and-responsibilities_en)
- Global Forest Watch Pro is the public pattern for this workflow. Companies upload farms, mills, or sourcing areas, then look at tree-cover loss, a 2020 forest layer, and imagery to decide where to investigate. WRI has described it as a way to prioritize traceability and due diligence, not as a traceability system and not as a certificate. Named early users include commodity traders and consumer-goods buyers (Cargill, Louis Dreyfus, Olam, Unilever, Mondelēz, P&G, Golden Agri-Resources). A 2020 WRI outcome note said about a third of users were civil society and local enforcement, and that financial institutions also used it. GFW Pro’s own user pages now tell food and agriculture businesses to measure forest loss after 2020 and to rank sourcing areas. — [WRI, 2019 release](https://www.wri.org/news/release-companies-can-now-quickly-and-accurately-monitor-deforestation-around-world); [WRI outcome, 13 April 2020](https://www.wri.org/outcomes/over-100-companies-monitor-commodity-related-deforestation-their-supply-chains-and); [GFW Pro on prioritizing due diligence](https://pro-news.globalforestwatch.org/news-events/3-ways-gfw-pro-can-help-commodity-processors-and-traders-go-deforestation-free); [GFW Pro users](https://pro-news.globalforestwatch.org/users); [Accountability Framework, monitoring tools](https://accountability-framework.org/take-action/for-companies/monitor-and-verify-performance/monitoring-tools/)
- The same buyer pattern shows up in paid monitoring. Cargill’s August 2023 release says Satelligence would monitor soy, palm oil, and cocoa “to deliver on the company’s sustainability commitments,” with an open method and a deforestation-free percentage. An ESA use-case page lists Unilever, Cargill, Nestlé, Mondelēz, and IKEA as Satelligence clients. Those names are evidence of who buys the service. They must not appear in the Clearcut demo. — [Cargill, 8 August 2023](https://www.cargill.com/2023/satelligence-deploys-anti-deforestation-solutions); [ESA Stakeholder Engagement Facility](https://sef.apex.esa.int/en/use-cases/eo-supply-chain-monitoring)
- SPOTT (ZSL) is a disclosure scorecard, not a plot checker the buyer runs. Its 2025 timber assessment found 18% of 100 companies disclose source countries, 4% report the share traceable to forest-management-unit level, and none publish georeferenced maps for all third-party units. ZSL’s point is that satellite tools fail if the buyer does not know the claimed source. A 2019 SPOTT note already told downstream buyers and financiers to demand maps, because they carry market and legal risk. — [SPOTT, 2025](https://www.spott.org/news/global-timber-markets-and-critical-forests-threatened-by-traceability-gaps/); [SPOTT spatial-data brief, 2019](https://www.spott.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2019/07/Spatial-data-transparency-in-tropical-forestry-SPOTT-2019.pdf)
- GFW Pro’s own palm-oil write-up says a loss pixel is a lead, not a land-use verdict: analysts then look at monthly imagery to see whether the forest label was right, whether the cause looked human, and whether land use actually changed. That is an evidence pack (location, date, picture, interpretation), opened from a list. — [GFW Pro](https://pro-news.globalforestwatch.org/news-events/3-ways-gfw-pro-can-help-commodity-processors-and-traders-go-deforestation-free)

### Inferences
- Build the demo for one person: a sustainability or procurement analyst at a buyer who already has a coordinate and has to decide whether to escalate. Call them the user out loud. Do not build for “NGOs,” “the EU,” or “auditors” as the primary screen, even though those groups would read the same pack.
- In five minutes they need, in this order: whose plot, what changed, when, what the land looks like now, the sentence they may repeat, and the sentence they may not. A dashboard of many suppliers answers a different question (where do I look next) and hides the only thing a judge can verify tonight.
- The honesty feature buyers already expect is a lead that does not confirm. Showing the inconclusive second box is the product, not a bug.

### Gaps
- No public price list was pulled for GFW Pro or Satelligence, so “who pays” is evidenced by who deploys the tools and who has the legal duty, not by invoice data.
- How often a buyer’s in-house team versus a hired auditor opens the imagery was not quantified.

## What claim is defensible, and what is overclaiming?

### Takeaway
The defensible sentence is about one plot’s land-use change, not about a supplier’s legal status. “This plot shows tree-cover loss after the EUDR cutoff that is consistent with conversion to a plantation” matches the regulation’s definition and the evidence in hand. “This supplier is EUDR non-compliant” does not. Compliance needs a product traced to the plot, a legality check, a negligible-risk judgment, and an authority. Clearcut has none of those.

### Cited Findings
- “Deforestation” in the regulation means conversion of forest to agricultural use, whether or not a person caused it. “Forest” means more than 0.5 hectares, trees higher than 5 metres, canopy over 10% (or able to reach that), and not land already mainly agricultural or urban. An agricultural plantation, including oil palm and other commodity plantations, is agricultural use and is excluded from “forest.” “Deforestation-free” means the commodity was not produced on land deforested after 31 December 2020. For wood, it also means no forest degradation after that date. Any deforestation on a declared plot disqualifies the commodities from that plot. — [Consolidated Regulation, Article 2(3)–(13) and Article 9(1)(d)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A02023R1115-20251226)
- Competent authorities must use those legal definitions, not a looser industry meaning of “deforestation.” — [Commission FAQ 1.22](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/publications/faq-eudr-implementation_en)
- The regulation also requires legality under the laws of the country of production. A plot can fail EUDR even when the clearing itself is the question, and a clearing can be the wrong kind of event (degradation of a forest that is still forest, or loss that is not conversion to agriculture). FAQ 1.16’s worked example: illegal clearing on one part of a property can taint legality for production on another part of the same property. — [Commission FAQ 1.16 and 1.29 heading](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/publications/faq-eudr-implementation_en); [Article 3](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A02023R1115-20251226)
- Mass-balance mixing with unknown or non-compliant volumes is not allowed. A shipment is tied to every plot that could have fed it. One photographed box does not identify a supplier’s product. — [Commission FAQ 1.4–1.6 and 1.17, as extracted](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/publications/faq-eudr-implementation_en)
- No satellite brand is prescribed, and no map is “the” compliance layer. The Commission says cross-checks against images or forest maps are allowed, and that tools such as a global forest map are not a definitive compliance source. — [FAQ 5.18 and the observatory answer near FAQ 9.10](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/publications/faq-eudr-implementation_en)
- Hansen-style tree-cover loss is what buyers already use as a screen, then confirm with imagery, because loss is not the same thing as EUDR deforestation. GFW Pro tells users to stack a 2020 forest map, a natural-versus-planted layer, and loss, then look at the picture. — [GFW Pro](https://pro-news.globalforestwatch.org/news-events/3-ways-gfw-pro-can-help-commodity-processors-and-traders-go-deforestation-free)

### Inferences
- Allowed on-screen verdict, for the confirmed box only: “Post-2020 tree-cover loss, consistent with forest-to-plantation conversion. Not a compliance finding.”
- Put the evidence under that sentence in three chips a non-specialist can read: when (Hansen: about 20% of the box lost in 2023, which is after 31 December 2020, and the loss layer ends in 2024), what it looked like (real Sentinel-2, September 2019 canopy versus August 2026 grid, roads, and smoke), what the model called it (plantation grid, roads, planting rows). The August 2026 image can show the current land use. It cannot be narrated as “Hansen detected 2026 clearing.”
- The second box stays on screen as “inconclusive.” If the app issues the same verdict there, the first verdict becomes theater.
- Overclaiming, in the words judges will punish:
  - A red badge that says non-compliant, illegal, banned, or “EUDR fail.”
  - Any real company name, mill name, or “this supplier.”
  - A supplier score, a percent-compliant number, or a portfolio of red and green dots.
  - Treating the vision-model caption as a legal land-use class, or hiding that one of two boxes did not confirm.
  - Implying the 2025–26 smoke is in the Hansen record.
  - A generated or beautified satellite image offered as evidence.
  - An NDVI chart as the reason for the verdict. The team already knows it does not separate rainforest from a mature plantation; putting it on the critical path tells a judge the science is the wrong layer.
  - Claiming the plot meets the legal forest test (0.5 ha, 5 m, 10% canopy, not already agriculture on 31 December 2020). The demo has not measured that. “Consistent with” is the honest verb.
- Voice Q&A, if asked “so is this supplier illegal?”: the recorded answer is no. The app saw one plot’s change. It did not trace a shipment, check local law, or decide negligible risk. Member State authorities do that.

### Gaps
- Whether the West Kalimantan box would meet the Article 2 forest definition on 31 December 2020 was not assessed. The notes assume the team will not claim that test.
- Indonesia’s country-benchmark class (low, standard, or high risk) was not checked. It changes how much due diligence an operator owes, not what this plot image shows.

## What should the screen show, in order, for a five-minute judge?

### Takeaway
One plot, one path, no menu. The judge should understand the picture before they hear the acronym. The last beat is the limit of the claim, including the box the model would not call.

### Cited Findings
- Buyers already work location, then loss, then a picture, then a human call on land use. GFW Pro’s published flow is upload, compare loss to a 2020 forest layer, then use imagery to judge cause and land-use change. — [GFW Pro](https://pro-news.globalforestwatch.org/news-events/3-ways-gfw-pro-can-help-commodity-processors-and-traders-go-deforestation-free)
- The legal cutoff a judge might half-remember is 31 December 2020, and the duty that makes the plot matter is geolocation in a due diligence file, not a public ranking. — [Article 2(13)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A02023R1115-20251226); [FAQ 1.1](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/publications/faq-eudr-implementation_en)
- The demo facts to bind to that flow: fictional supplier, real coordinates near −0.78, 110.08 in West Kalimantan; September 2019 intact; August 2026 plantation grid, roads, and smoke; Hansen about 20% of the box lost in 2023; vision model described forest-to-plantation conversion; a second box did not confirm. Hansen does not contain 2025–26 clearing. Images used as evidence must be the real ones.

### Inferences
Run this sequence and nothing else. Target about 3 minutes of clicking and 2 minutes of questions.

1. Title card, one breath. “Clearcut. One fictional supplier. One real plot in West Kalimantan. We will not tell you they are EUDR non-compliant.” Show the coordinates and the cutoff date 31 December 2020. Show the application line in small type: main duties 30 December 2026; most micro and small operators 30 June 2027; delayed twice.
2. The picture, full width, before any chart. Left: September 2019, caption “canopy.” Right: August 2026, caption “grid, roads, smoke.” Label both as Sentinel-2, not as illustrations. A judge with no forestry background should see the change before the method.
3. When. One number: about 20% of this box lost in 2023, which is after the cutoff. One caveat on the same card: “Loss map ends in 2024. The 2026 image is not a 2026 Hansen detection.”
4. What it became. The vision-model phrase, tied to the real August 2026 frame: plantation grid, roads, planting rows. Do not show a second, generated picture.
5. The sentence they may say, in a box: “This plot shows post-2020 tree-cover loss consistent with plantation conversion.” Under it, three strikes: no link from this plot to a shipment; no legality check; not an authority’s decision.
6. The second box, same layout, verdict “Inconclusive.” This is the credibility beat. Skip it and the app looks like it always finds a plantation.
7. Stop. If they ask a question, play the recorded answer. Do not open a map of Borneo, a supplier list, or a settings panel.

### Gaps
- No user test of this sequence was run. The order is inferred from how buyers already confirm a lead and from the five-minute constraint.

## What should be cut so the team ships a demo?

### Takeaway
Ship the evidence pack for two precomputed boxes and a recorded answer. Cut every feature that implies a platform, a live satellite call, a legal determination, or a second scientific method.

### Cited Findings
- Downstream SMEs do not file due diligence statements, and even large downstream firms verify only on a substantiated concern. A demo that files a mock EU statement, or that scores a whole supplier book, is implementing a job the 2025 amendment removed from most of the chain. — [Green Forum, roles](https://green-forum.ec.europa.eu/nature-and-biodiversity/implementing-eu-deforestation-regulation-eudr/roles-and-responsibilities_en)
- The Commission leaves the choice of imagery open and does not certify a model. Extra datasets on screen do not make the claim more legal. — [FAQ 5.18](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/publications/faq-eudr-implementation_en)
- The given constraints: offline except voice, and voice needs a recorded fallback; fictional supplier; no generated images as evidence; Hansen stops at 2024; NDVI is the wrong main signal.

### Inferences
Cut, even if someone already started it:

- Any live map, tile server, or model call. Precompute both boxes, including the inconclusive one.
- Accounts, uploads, “add your supplier,” and a portfolio dashboard. One fictional supplier, two boxes.
- Real company, mill, or trader names. Anywhere, including the voice script.
- A compliance color, a risk score, and the words non-compliant, illegal, violation, and certified.
- A mock due diligence statement, Information System login, or country-risk map.
- NDVI time series, a second forest definition, and any generated image.
- Alerts, 2025–26 loss claims, and a story that the smoke is dated by Hansen.
- Legality, chain of custody, smallholder traceability, and auditor workflow.
- Free-form voice. Record the refusal answer and one short answer to “what am I looking at?” If the network fails, the demo still ends.

Keep: the before/after, the 2023 loss figure, the model’s land-use phrase, the allowed sentence, the inconclusive box, the EUDR date line with the uncertainty, and the recorded fallback.

### Gaps
- None on the cut list itself. It follows from the legal claim boundary and the one-night constraint, not from a missing source.
