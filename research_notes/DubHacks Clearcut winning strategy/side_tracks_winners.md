# DubHacks 2026 non-sponsored tracks and 2025 winner patterns

Scope note: today is 17 October 2026, during DubHacks 2026 (17–18 October 2026). Track rules below are 2026. Winner examples are DubHacks 2025 (18–19 October 2025, 751 participants, “$50,000 in cash” on the Devpost header). The 2025 Devpost gallery is behind Cloudflare from this environment, so video and write-up stats are a convenience sample of pages that loaded, not a census of every winner.

## Every 2026 non-sponsored track: prompt, prize, judging

### Takeaway
The 2026 non-sponsored page lists 11 tracks and says a team may enter as many of them as it wants. Only DubHacks NEXT has a published cash amount ($200). Most MLH prizes are hardware or swag, and most of those tracks are judged from Devpost rather than a guaranteed table visit.

### Cited Findings
- Teams may submit to as many non-sponsored side tracks as they want, on top of exactly one title track and at most three sponsored side tracks. — [‘26 Non-Sponsored Side Tracks](https://dubhacks.notion.site/26-Non-Sponsored-Side-Tracks-cc7ff32c1d7e83f7a1c9818214afba7f); [‘26 Submissions and Judging Overview](https://dubhacks.notion.site/14eff32c1d7e837793e2018a30a234cb)
- That non-sponsored page was created 13 October 2026 and last edited 16 October 2026 (Notion `created_time` / `last_edited_time`). — Notion `loadCachedPageChunk` for page `cc7ff32c-1d7e-83f7-a1c9-818214afba7f`
- In-person side-track judging is limited to DubHacks NEXT, T-Mobile, Cloudforce, SideShift, Perceptron, Photon, and VEYZL. “All other tracks will use your devpost + main track judging to determine winners.” — [‘26 Submissions and Judging Overview](https://dubhacks.notion.site/14eff32c1d7e837793e2018a30a234cb)
- MLH “will primarily judge projects through Devpost submissions but may visit select teams in person.” Not every MLH submitter gets a visit, and submissions are still eligible. — same judging overview
- Main-track judging is expo-style in the HUB Ballroom: three 50-minute rounds, teams stay at an assigned table, and side-track judges who do come walk to the table. Presentations should stay under 3 minutes so there is time for Q&A. A Devpost video is mandatory or the team “will not be allowed to judge.” — same judging overview
- Official rubric used for that judging (8-point scales): Functionality 30%, Track applicability 30%, Design 20%, Creativity 20%. Excellent functionality means the project “successfully works.” Excellent track applicability means the project “goes above and beyond” as a demonstration of the track. — [‘26: Official Judges Rubric](https://dubhacks.notion.site/dh-26-judges-rubric)
- Hackathon code rule: at most 30% of code may be code not written during the hackathon, and at least 70% must be written during it. Anything presented at judging should only have been worked on from 12:30 p.m. Saturday to 10:00 a.m. Sunday. — [The DubHacks '26 Hacker Guide](https://dubhacks.notion.site/15dff32c1d7e83748aac8154036aa658)

### Inferences
- For a deforestation due-diligence demo, the tracks that match the product without a fake integration are NEXT and Commit Fellowship (a buyer, a payment, and a reason to keep building). GoDaddy is a near-free extra if the demo has a real domain. Tiger Data, Vultr, and MongoDB are worth it only if that system actually runs the demo. Gemini is worth it only if the demo calls Gemini. Solana and Backboard are a poor fit unless the product truly needs chain settlement or cross-session model memory.
- Freshman is closed unless every member is a freshman, and the 2026 prize is unpublished, so it is not a reason to enter.
- DEV is a post-weekend $100 editorial prize. It does not compete with Sunday’s table time.
- Cash on this whole list that is actually quantified is $200 (NEXT first) plus $100 (DEV). Hardware prizes are unnamed in dollar value.

### Gaps
- No 2026 prize is stated for Best Freshman Beginner’s Project. In 2025 the same track’s Devpost listing said “First Prize: Amazon Echo Pop.” That is a 2025 prize, not a 2026 one. — [DubHacks '25 Devpost](https://dubhacks25.devpost.com/)
- GoDaddy gift-card amount, Stream Deck quantity, and Vultr unit count are not stated.
- Solana’s prize name disagrees between the DubHacks Notion card and the MLH challenges page. Neither page says which text wins.
- The track cards do not publish a separate MLH scorecard beyond Tiger Data’s “innovative, impactful, and performance-driven” line and DEV’s editorial criteria.

## NEXT, Tiger Data, Vultr, GoDaddy, Commit Fellowship versus a precomputed Python pipeline and a small web demo

### Takeaway
A small web demo can qualify for NEXT, Commit Fellowship, and GoDaddy. Tiger Data and Vultr require the demo to actually run on those products. A pipeline finished before 12:30 p.m. Saturday also collides with the 70/30 code rule if that pipeline is most of what is shown.

### Cited Findings
- **NEXT requirements** are the five bullets above (user, builder energy, weekend iteration, revenue, intent to continue). No specific stack, dataset, or video-only path is required. It is judged in person. First prize is $200 plus a Batch 6 interview; second is the interview only. — [non-sponsored page](https://dubhacks.notion.site/26-Non-Sponsored-Side-Tracks-cc7ff32c1d7e83f7a1c9818214afba7f); [judging overview](https://dubhacks.notion.site/14eff32c1d7e837793e2018a30a234cb)
- **Tiger Data** does not list a minimum SDK, a required table schema, or a required continuous aggregate. It does say the winner is whoever shows Tiger Data powering the project, and it advertises continuous aggregates as the way to serve charts. The same paragraph appears on other MLH events with prize label “Stream Deck Mini.” Submissions on those MLH pages are “only open during the event.” — [HackPrinceton challenge](https://www.mlh.com/events/hackprinceton-fall-2026/challenges/019fcfdb-abbc-d470-b762-e73f1ee414d2); [non-sponsored page](https://dubhacks.notion.site/26-Non-Sponsored-Side-Tracks-cc7ff32c1d7e83f7a1c9818214afba7f)
- **Vultr** tells teams to sign up, claim free cloud credits, and take the hack to the cloud, using scalable compute and/or Cloud GPUs. It does not require a GPU. Prize label on the DubHacks card is M5Stack Official Tab5. — [non-sponsored page](https://dubhacks.notion.site/26-Non-Sponsored-Side-Tracks-cc7ff32c1d7e83f7a1c9818214afba7f); [MLH prizes](https://www.mlh.com/events/dubhacks-97/prizes)
- **GoDaddy Registry** requires registering a domain “with GoDaddy Registry.” No app feature is specified. Prize: “Digital Gift Card,” amount unpublished. — [non-sponsored page](https://dubhacks.notion.site/26-Non-Sponsored-Side-Tracks-cc7ff32c1d7e83f7a1c9818214afba7f)
- **Commit Fellowship** qualify line, quoted from the page: “building a working prototype with a team, demonstrate a real problem worth solving, and opt into the Commit Fellowship Prize Category when you submit,” then submit [this supplemental form](https://mlh.link/commit-fellowship-form). Selection is after the event, by email. Benefits described: a chance to interview for a 3-week MLH and Transcend Network sprint, live coaching, peer code reviews, customer-discovery sessions, and, after completion, access to “thousands of dollars” in microgrants and tool credits. The hackathon prize itself is the interview plus a copy of *The Lean Startup*. — [non-sponsored page](https://dubhacks.notion.site/26-Non-Sponsored-Side-Tracks-cc7ff32c1d7e83f7a1c9818214afba7f); [MLH challenge](https://www.mlh.com/events/dubhacks-97/challenges/01a10d10-ad0f-a22c-bc1a-71ba1cc64c1b)
- The form URL returned HTTP 500 when fetched on 17 October 2026, so the form fields could not be read. — fetch of `https://mlh.link/commit-fellowship-form`
- Code-origin rule and judging window are as cited in the previous section. Final Devpost submissions are due 10:00 a.m. Sunday and “must include a video submission.” A Saturday 10:30 p.m. draft with tracks declared is mandatory. — [hacker guide](https://dubhacks.notion.site/15dff32c1d7e83748aac8154036aa658); [DubHacks '26 Devpost](https://dubhacks-2026.devpost.com/)

### Inferences
- **NEXT:** a precomputed pipeline plus a small web demo can meet the written bar if the live pitch shows a real buyer (for due diligence: a procurement, finance, or compliance user), what they would pay for, and what changed during the weekend. The demo has to work in the room. Precomputing the forest analysis before the event is a rules problem if that code is most of the repo, because of the 70/30 rule and the “only worked on from 12:30 p.m. Saturday” line.
- **Tiger Data:** a static site that only displays a Python script’s precomputed files does not match “how Tiger Data powers your project.” Loading those results into Tiger Data and having the web demo query them, especially through a continuous aggregate or a time-series of alerts, does match the prompt. The aggregate is advertised, not listed as a checkbox.
- **Vultr:** the laptop pipeline alone does not match “take your next hack to the cloud.” Serving the web demo, or running a step of the pipeline, on a Vultr instance does. A GPU is optional in the text.
- **GoDaddy:** the pipeline is irrelevant. A domain registered with GoDaddy Registry and pointed at the demo is the stated requirement. This is the lowest-effort eligible add-on.
- **Commit Fellowship:** the same working web demo can meet “working prototype” and “real problem” if it is submitted as a team, the category is opted into, and the supplemental form is sent. A solo submission conflicts with the words “with a team.” The prize is an interview, selected after the event, not a scored Sunday table prize.

### Gaps
- No public checklist says Tiger Data must be the system of record versus a demo database, or that Vultr must host the whole app versus one service.
- Commit Fellowship form fields, deadline, and whether “team” means two or more people are not on the Notion card. The form URL errored.
- Whether MLH staff can see code that never ran on Tiger Data or Vultr, if the Devpost text claims they did, is not documented.

## Organizer advice on expo pitches

### Takeaway
2026 organizers tell teams to pitch at the table for under 3 minutes inside a 50-minute round, not a published 5-minute slot. Judges come to the team. MLH may not come at all.

### Cited Findings
- “For your presentation, please try your best to keep your presentation under 3 minutes so there’s time for Q&A.” Judges “will cut you off.” Teams must also record that presentation on Devpost, and “TEAMS WHO DO NOT SUBMIT A VIDEO WILL NOT BE ALLOWED TO JUDGE.” — [‘26 Submissions and Judging Overview](https://dubhacks.notion.site/14eff32c1d7e837793e2018a30a234cb)
- Logistics on that page: judging workshop 10:30 a.m. Sunday 18 October 2026 in the HUB Ballroom, arrive about 10:20 a.m., judging lasts “roughly 3–4 hours,” three rounds of 50 minutes with 10-minute transitions. Each team is assigned one judging round. In that slot they are judged on the main track and any side tracks. Main-track teams go to an assigned table and stay there. Side-track judges “will also come to their tables” and “can come at any time.” — same page
- “The only side tracks doing in-person judging are DubHacks NEXT, T-Mobile, Cloudforce, SideShift, Perceptron, Photon, and VEYZL.” Other tracks use Devpost plus main-track judging. MLH may still visit some teams and will not visit all of them. — same page
- A separate hacker-guide line says judging “will be in-person expo-style, in judging rooms, or recorded online,” and at least one person must be present. Another line says “Judging is entirely in-person EXCEPT for Visa projects that have NO side track,” with Visa using a 3-minute Devpost video. — [hacker guide](https://dubhacks.notion.site/15dff32c1d7e83748aac8154036aa658); [judging overview](https://dubhacks.notion.site/14eff32c1d7e837793e2018a30a234cb)
- The public schedule on the 2026 site lists Sunday judging as 11:30 a.m.–2:00 p.m., while the hacker-guide page says a 10:30 a.m. workshop and roughly 3–4 hours of judging. — [dh26.dubhacks.co](https://dh26.dubhacks.co/); [judging overview](https://dubhacks.notion.site/14eff32c1d7e837793e2018a30a234cb)
- 2026 Devpost repeats the four rubric weights and says “some side tracks will use a video submission in place of an in-person judging round. Please check your hacker guide.” — [DubHacks '26 Devpost](https://dubhacks-2026.devpost.com/)

### Inferences
- The “about 5 minutes, judges walk table to table” picture is close to the 2026 format and wrong on the clock. Organizers want the talk under 3 minutes so Q&A fits, inside a 50-minute window in which several judges may arrive separately. NEXT is one of the tracks that does walk up. Tiger Data, Vultr, GoDaddy, and Commit Fellowship are in the “Devpost, maybe a visit” group.
- A deforestation team should plan the spoken NEXT pitch around a working click-through and a payer, and put the track-specific proof (Tiger queries, Vultr URL, domain, Commit form) in the Devpost, because those judges may never stand at the table.

### Gaps
- No 2025 organizer post was found that sets a 5-minute expo pitch. Searches returned other hackathons’ expo rules (HackDFW’s 3+2, Technica’s 2+2, CruzHacks’ 4+3), which are not DubHacks.
- The hacker guide’s “judging rooms or recorded online” line and the ballroom table procedure are both on official 2026 pages and are not reconciled in one paragraph. Visa-only video judging is explicit; it does not say MLH tracks are video-only.
- The public website schedule (11:30 a.m.–2:00 p.m.) and the hacker guide (10:30 a.m. workshop plus 3–4 hours) disagree. The guide is the more detailed organizer document.
