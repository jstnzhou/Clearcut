Primary pages opened: [Hacker Guide](https://dubhacks.notion.site/The-DubHacks-26-Hacker-Guide-15dff32c1d7e83748aac8154036aa658) (including child pages for [submissions and judging](https://www.notion.so/14eff32c1d7e837793e2018a30a234cb), [official rubric](https://www.notion.so/154ff32c1d7e8304a76b011636f6732f), [title tracks](https://www.notion.so/bbaff32c1d7e8308be7d8185bd983487), [sponsored side tracks](https://www.notion.so/5e3ff32c1d7e834d9844812256c9fa06), [non-sponsored side tracks](https://www.notion.so/cc7ff32c1d7e83f7a1c9818214afba7f), [prizes](https://www.notion.so/7f2ff32c1d7e83cb22981e0c329c033), [Visa rubric](https://www.notion.so/3efff32c1d7e809d9063de7d7157d957)), [Devpost](https://dubhacks-2026.devpost.com/), [Devpost rules](https://dubhacks-2026.devpost.com/rules), [Devpost dates](https://dubhacks-2026.devpost.com/details/dates), [dh26.dubhacks.co](https://dh26.dubhacks.co/). The guide’s top callout says the page “is still being updated.” All guide times are labeled Pacific Daylight Time.

## What are the exact deadlines for the Devpost draft (Sat Oct 17, 2026) and final submission (Sun Oct 18, 2026), and what must each contain?

### Takeaway
The Hacker Guide and Devpost agree on a Saturday 10:30 PM PDT draft and a Sunday 10:00 AM PDT final Devpost submission, but the public event site says submissions are due at 10:30 AM Sunday. A missing Saturday draft blocks Sunday judging. The final must include a recorded presentation video or the team “will not be allowed to judge.”

### Cited Findings
- Draft, Devpost wording: “a draft of your project is submitted to Devpost Saturday, 10/17 before 10:30 PM.” It must “add all members of your team as contributors (including their Discords), and also declare the tracks you'll be submitting to.” “If you do not submit a draft on Saturday night, you will not be able to participate in judging on Sunday.” Final: “due Sunday, 10/18, before 10:00 AM.” Banner: “Deadline: Oct 18, 2026 @ 10:00am PDT.” — [Devpost](https://dubhacks-2026.devpost.com/)
- Devpost’s machine schedule window is “Submissions: October 17 at 8:00am PDT” through “October 18 at 10:00am PDT.” “Winners Announced” ends “October 18 at 4:00pm PDT.” The line “October 18 at 1:00pm EDT” on the same page is the same instant as 10:00 AM PDT. — [Devpost dates](https://dubhacks-2026.devpost.com/details/dates); [Devpost](https://dubhacks-2026.devpost.com/)
- Guide, Draft/Project Submissions: “Hackers MUST make an initial submission by 10:30 PM on Saturday.” Finals “are due sharply at 10:00 AM.” The draft “is solely just to understand how long the submission process will take and work.” Separately: “Final submissions must be made by 10 AM on Sunday and must include a video submission.” Saturday schedule row: “Initial Devpost Submissions DUE” at 10:30 PM. Sunday row: “Hacking Ends, Final submissions due on Devpost” at 10:00 AM. — [Hacker Guide](https://dubhacks.notion.site/The-DubHacks-26-Hacker-Guide-15dff32c1d7e83748aac8154036aa658)
- Submissions overview: hacking begins “12:30 PM on Saturday, October 17th.” Teams have until “10:00 AM on Sunday, October 18th.” “This is a HARD deadline, and Devpost will be locked after this point” so teams “cannot edit their submissions.” Draft “by 10:30 PM” must include “an overview of what the project is and the tracks the team is submitting their project to.” — [Submissions and Judging Overview](https://www.notion.so/14eff32c1d7e837793e2018a30a234cb)
- Video is part of the final, not described as part of the draft: “We also require you to record your presentation and submit it to Devpost.” “TEAMS WHO DO NOT SUBMIT A VIDEO WILL NOT BE ALLOWED TO JUDGE.” The video is so teams can “rehearse” and so “judges can use this as a supplementary resource when making final decisions.” No general video length is stated. Visa’s track uses a separate “3-minute video.” — [Submissions and Judging Overview](https://www.notion.so/14eff32c1d7e837793e2018a30a234cb)
- Code must be “uploaded BEFORE the deadline and linked to Devpost.” “project demos with a working product yet have no code uploaded will be disqualified.” — [Hacker Guide](https://dubhacks.notion.site/The-DubHacks-26-Hacker-Guide-15dff32c1d7e83748aac8154036aa658)
- Conflicting public schedule: dh26.dubhacks.co lists Sunday “10:30AM Submissions Due!” and judging “11:30AM - 2:00PM,” closing “2:15PM - 3:05PM.” — [dh26.dubhacks.co](https://dh26.dubhacks.co/); contradicted by the 10:00 AM final in [Devpost](https://dubhacks-2026.devpost.com/) and the [Hacker Guide](https://dubhacks.notion.site/The-DubHacks-26-Hacker-Guide-15dff32c1d7e83748aac8154036aa658)

### Inferences
- Treat 10:30 PM Saturday and 10:00 AM Sunday as the operative Devpost locks, because both the guide and Devpost say so and the guide calls 10:00 AM a hard Devpost lock. The site’s 10:30 AM “Submissions Due” matches the guide’s judging-workshop start, not the Devpost lock.
- Draft content is stricter on Devpost (every member, Discord handles, tracks) and on the judging page (project overview plus tracks) than the guide sentence that calls the draft only a timing rehearsal. Satisfying the stricter wording is what the pages say is required to stay eligible.

### Gaps
- No page states a required runtime for the general (non-Visa) Devpost video, or a required GitHub README outline, screenshot list, or demo URL field.
- Whether Devpost’s software lock is exactly 10:00:00 AM PDT, or “before 10:00 AM,” is not specified beyond those phrases.

## What does the Hacker Guide say about team size, one-project rule, 70/30 code rule, public GitHub, Discord handles, tracks, expo judging, video judging, and prizes?

### Takeaway
Max 4 people, one project per hacker, only this hackathon, at most 30% pre-written code, public GitHub recommended, exactly one title track plus up to 3 sponsored side tracks. Judging is in-person expo at assigned tables except Visa-only projects, which are a 3-minute video. At least one teammate must be present to win a prize, and a Devpost video is required to be judged at all.

### Cited Findings
- Team size: “4 hackers maximum per team (anything less including 1 is okay…).” — [Hacker Guide](https://dubhacks.notion.site/The-DubHacks-26-Hacker-Guide-15dff32c1d7e83748aac8154036aa658)
- One project: “Each hacker is only allowed to be a part of and create one project during this hackathon.” — [Hacker Guide](https://dubhacks.notion.site/The-DubHacks-26-Hacker-Guide-15dff32c1d7e83748aac8154036aa658)
- Cross-hackathon: “Only submit to DubHacks '26 — projects that submit to other hackathons will be disqualified.” — [Hacker Guide](https://dubhacks.notion.site/The-DubHacks-26-Hacker-Guide-15dff32c1d7e83748aac8154036aa658)
- 70/30: “Public frameworks and libraries are allowed, but submitting mostly copied code will result in project disqualification. We run off the 70-30 rule, where AT MOST 30% of your code can be code not written during the hackathon, and AT LEAST 70% of the code must be yours, written during this hackathon.” — [Hacker Guide](https://dubhacks.notion.site/The-DubHacks-26-Hacker-Guide-15dff32c1d7e83748aac8154036aa658)
- GitHub: “The recommend way to upload code is to a PUBLIC Github repository.” Missing uploaded code linked on Devpost disqualifies even a working demo. — [Hacker Guide](https://dubhacks.notion.site/The-DubHacks-26-Hacker-Guide-15dff32c1d7e83748aac8154036aa658)

## How does the Devpost page describe submission requirements, judging criteria, and schedule?

### Takeaway
Devpost requires the Saturday draft (members, Discords, tracks) and a Sunday 10:00 AM PDT final, scores Functionality 30%, Track Applicability 30%, Design 20%, Creativity 20%, and points teams to the Hacker Guide rubric. Its own schedule window ends 10:00 AM PDT Sunday. It does not list table times.

### Cited Findings
- Eligibility on Devpost: “Ages 18+ only,” “College students only,” “All countries/territories, excluding standard exceptions.” — [Devpost](https://dubhacks-2026.devpost.com/)
- Devpost rules page repeats only four lines: be kind and respectful and links the MLH code of conduct (https://static.mlh.io/docs/mlh-code-of-conduct.pdf); “Teams may have up to four members”; projects “started and completed during” the event; wear badges for “building entry.” — [Devpost rules](https://dubhacks-2026.devpost.com/rules)
- “What To Build”: exactly one title track, up to three sponsored side tracks, unlimited non-sponsored. “Most of our tracks are very open ended.” — [Devpost](https://dubhacks-2026.devpost.com/)
- “What to Submit”: draft before 10:30 PM Saturday with contributors “including their Discords” and declared tracks, or no Sunday judging; final “before 10:00 AM” Sunday. “Some side tracks will use a video submission in place of an in-person judging round. Please check your hacker guide.” — [Devpost](https://dubhacks-2026.devpost.com/)
- Criteria, each line says “Refer to rubric on the Hacker Guide”: Functionality “Code Functionality and Quality of Execution (30%)”; Track Applicability “How related projects are to their chosen track (30%)”; Design “User Interface/Aesthetics (20%)”; Creativity “Unique/New Solution to a Problem with Little Weaknesses (20%).” — [Devpost](https://dubhacks-2026.devpost.com/)
- The guide rubric matches those weights and scores each 1–8: Excellent 7–8, Good 5–6, Fair 3–4, Insufficient 1–2. Functionality excellent = “successfully works” with “robust code” and “high quality of code execution.” Design excellent = “very easy to understand and looks very professional.” Creativity excellent = “Complex and seemingly addresses all angles… in a very unique manner”; insufficient = “merely replicates existing approaches.” Track excellent = “goes above and beyond” and is “an exemplary demonstration of the track's focus”; insufficient = “unrelated” and “doesn’t reference it at all.” — [Official Judges Rubric](https://www.notion.so/154ff32c1d7e8304a76b011636f6732f)
- Visa’s separate rubric: Innovation 25%, User Experience 25%, Technical Execution 20% (“How effectively has the team implemented AI”), Commerce Impact 15%, Trust & Responsibility 15% (privacy, transparency, security, “responsible use of AI”). — [Visa Judging Rubric](https://www.notion.so/3efff32c1d7e809d9063de7d7157d957)
- Devpost prize labels use “Chef's Kitchen” for the same 1st/2nd physical pools the guide names under title tracks and calls “Chef’s Choice” on the tracks page. — [Devpost](https://dubhacks-2026.devpost.com/); contradicted on the name by [Title Tracks](https://www.notion.so/bbaff32c1d7e8308be7d8185bd983487)
- Devpost cash line is “$ 9,200 in cash” and “$ 9,200+ in prizes + other prizes.” The marketing site says “$44,000+ In Prizes.” — [Devpost](https://dubhacks-2026.devpost.com/); [dh26.dubhacks.co](https://dh26.dubhacks.co/)
- Devpost lists one judge, “Lisa Kapil, Visa.” — [Devpost](https://dubhacks-2026.devpost.com/)
- A DubHacks LinkedIn post (Oct 6, 2026) said applications closed “Wednesday, October 7th, at 11:59 PM PST,” “undergraduate students only,” and “18+ by October 17th.” That is narrower than Devpost’s “College students only.” — [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/dubhacks_only-two-days-remaining-to-apply-to-our-hackathon-activity-7513075365947314177-jIoC); [Devpost](https://dubhacks-2026.devpost.com/)

### Inferences
- The four Devpost “rules” are a short mirror. The operative rule set is the Hacker Guide, which Devpost tells teams to use for the rubric and for which tracks are video-judged.
- Weighting is consistent between Devpost and the 2026 rubric (30/30/20/20). Each criterion is also marked “(8 Points),” so the points and the percents are two labels on the same four rows, not an extra scoring system.

### Gaps
- Devpost does not publish round times, table assignments, or a per-team pitch length. Those are only in the guide.
- Devpost’s Cloudforce block lists “Most Viral $1,000” and a “Raffle” without the guide’s “Most Interesting $500” and raffle “$500.” SpaceXAI’s Cursor keyboard prize is on the prize page and not in the Devpost prize list that was retrieved. Which listing is current if they diverge was not confirmed.

## Any rules about AI use, pre-existing code, APIs, or what must be demoable in person?

### Takeaway
There is no general ban or special permission statement for AI coding tools. Pre-existing work is limited by the start-at-12:30 rule and the 70/30 code rule. Public libraries and APIs are allowed. Some sponsor tracks require a specific API or tool. In person, teams need a person at judging, a sub-3-minute pitch, and code that was uploaded; a working demo with no repo is a disqualification.

### Cited Findings
- No sentence in the rules, judging overview, or Devpost rules restricts ChatGPT, Copilot, Cursor, or other AI coding tools. The guide’s Technical Resources page is stack advice (React, Flask, Firebase, Stripe, Twilio, and similar) and does not mention an AI-use policy. — [Hacker Guide](https://dubhacks.notion.site/The-DubHacks-26-Hacker-Guide-15dff32c1d7e83748aac8154036aa658); [Technical Resources](https://www.notion.so/080ff32c1d7e8255b64281a7c049029a)
- Pre-existing work: nothing presented “should only be worked on from 12:30 PM on Saturday to 10 AM on Sunday”; “no coding or building before 12:30 PM”; ideation before that is allowed; “AT MOST 30%” of code may be “not written during the hackathon.” “Public frameworks and libraries are allowed.” — [Hacker Guide](https://dubhacks.notion.site/The-DubHacks-26-Hacker-Guide-15dff32c1d7e83748aac8154036aa658)
- Track-specific tool rules, not general rules: Visa solutions should be “powered by Generative AI.” SpaceXAI: “your project must be built with Cursor. The more you use Cursor, the more likely you are to win. You must also use the Grok Imagine or Voice API.” Bonus for “Grok Bot.” VEYZL: build “an AI-powered project,” run “a real VEYZL audit” on a GitHub pull request, and show that findings “changed the build.” Photon: “must be built on Photon 2.0 and live on at least one Photon channel.” Perceptron perception uses their API; robotics requires the workshop and a shared SO-101 arm. T-Mobile: “synthetic data only. No real customer, account, or transaction data.” — [Title Tracks](https://www.notion.so/bbaff32c1d7e8308be7d8185bd983487); [Sponsored Side Tracks](https://www.notion.so/5e3ff32c1d7e834d9844812256c9fa06)
- What must be demoable: a working demo with “no code uploaded” is disqualified. Rubric top score requires the project “successfully works.” “at least 1 person be present at judging to qualify for a prize.” “your entire team is on-site for judging” is “of the UTMOST IMPORTANCE.” Pitch “under 3 minutes so there’s time for Q&A”; “judges… will cut you off.” Teams “stay at their tables for the duration of the round” because side-track judges “can come at any time.” — [Hacker Guide](https://dubhacks.notion.site/The-DubHacks-26-Hacker-Guide-15dff32c1d7e83748aac8154036aa658); [Official Judges Rubric](https://www.notion.so/154ff32c1d7e8304a76b011636f6732f); [Submissions and Judging Overview](https://www.notion.so/14eff32c1d7e837793e2018a30a234cb)
- Cloudforce “Most Viral” is “Highest real engagement, live at judging,” so that prize is scored on something visible during judging, not only on the Devpost page. — [Prizes](https://www.notion.so/7f2ff32c1d7e83cb22981e0c329c033)

### Inferences
- Using an API or a public library does not, by itself, count as the 30% “code not written during the hackathon.” The 30% rule is about copied project code. The pages do not define how they measure the percentage.
- AI-generated code is not classified. If organizers later treat it as “not written” by the team, that would be an interpretation the current text does not state.

### Gaps
- No written rule was found on whether AI-generated code counts toward the 70%, whether starters/templates must be disclosed, or whether a live demo must succeed versus a recorded fallback during the table visit. The 2025 recap below describes a video fallback; it is not a 2026 rule.

## What is the judging format (table visits, time per team, video vs live) and what past guidance exists about winning pitches?

### Takeaway
Main-track judging is expo-style in the HUB Ballroom: one assigned 50-minute round, judges come to the table, pitches should stay under 3 minutes, and a Devpost video is mandatory. The public site’s judging block (11:30 AM–2:00 PM) does not match the guide’s workshop-plus-3-to-4-hour window. The only concrete “what won” pitch notes are a 2025 first-place participant write-up, not organizer advice.

### Cited Findings
- Format: main tracks (Community Kitchen, Chef’s Choice, Cook Smarter) are judged in the HUB Ballroom. Teams go to an “assigned table” and “must stay for the duration of the judging round.” Side-track judges “come to their tables” and “can come at any time.” Teams with no side tracks “can leave the ballroom” after the main-track judge. If a side-track judge has not arrived, “DO NOT LEAVE THE BALLROOM.” — [Submissions and Judging Overview](https://www.notion.so/14eff32c1d7e837793e2018a30a234cb)
- Time structure: “a judging workshop at 10:30 AM on Sunday” then “judging will start immediately after, lasting roughly 3-4 hours.” “arrive at the venue at around 10:20 AM.” “Judging includes 3, 50 minute long rounds, with a 10 minute transition in between each one.” Each team gets “exactly ONE round.” A second round is assigned “to eat lunch.” Sunday schedule rows: workshop “10:30 - 11:00 AM” in the Ballroom; “MEAL + JUDGING” starts “11:15 AM”; closing “2:45 - 3:30 PM.” — [Submissions and Judging Overview](https://www.notion.so/14eff32c1d7e837793e2018a30a234cb); [Hacker Guide](https://dubhacks.notion.site/The-DubHacks-26-Hacker-Guide-15dff32c1d7e83748aac8154036aa658)
- The public site instead says Sunday judging “11:30AM - 2:00PM” and closing “2:15PM - 3:05PM.” — [dh26.dubhacks.co](https://dh26.dubhacks.co/); contradicted by the guide times above
- Schedule source: “judging schedule by accessing their hacker profile shortly after submissions close.” “Teams are randomly distributed.” “IMPERATIVE that teams constantly check their hacker profile.” Profile URL in the guide: http://profile.dubhacks.co/ — [Submissions and Judging Overview](https://www.notion.so/14eff32c1d7e837793e2018a30a234cb); [Hacker Guide](https://dubhacks.notion.site/The-DubHacks-26-Hacker-Guide-15dff32c1d7e83748aac8154036aa658)
- Pitch length: “keep your presentation under 3 minutes so there’s time for Q&A.” Judges “have a very strict schedule” and “will cut you off.” — [Submissions and Judging Overview](https://www.notion.so/14eff32c1d7e837793e2018a30a234cb)
- Missed slot: organizers “will try to allocate you a new judge” in an “overflow round” but it is “not guaranteed.” “In round 7 of each phase” missed teams raise hands. “Do not begin presenting until we announce the start of the round.” Do not present in leftover time. “DO NOT try to insert your presentation where you see fit… could lead to your team getting removed altogether.” — [Submissions and Judging Overview](https://www.notion.so/14eff32c1d7e837793e2018a30a234cb)

### Inferences
- The 50 minutes is the team’s window at the table, not the length of one judge’s visit. The only stated speaking target is under 3 minutes plus Q&A, and judges may cut that short.
- “Round 7 of each phase” does not match the “3 rounds” description. It may be leftover copy. The operative instruction is: if you miss the slot, wait for an announced overflow rather than pitching on your own.
- 2025’s encouraged 2-minute sponsor video is not the 2026 rule. 2026 requires a video for everyone and specifies 3 minutes only for Visa.

### Gaps
- Minutes per individual judge visit are not stated.
- Whether the recorded video must be the same demo as the live pitch, and whether judges watch it before walking the floor, is not stated. The overview only calls it a “supplementary resource” for “final decisions,” plus a hard gate on being allowed to judge.
- T-Mobile and Cloudforce embedded briefs were not opened, so their judging checklists are unknown.
- The site FAQ anchor (https://dh26.dubhacks.co/#faq) is a sponsor Q&A (history, demographics, recruiting), not hacker rules.
